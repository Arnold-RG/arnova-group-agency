BUSINESS OPERATIONS · PRACTICAL GUIDE

Five simple ways to keep track of your shop's sales.

You don't need a big system to start understanding where the money goes.

If you run a shop or salon, every day can get busy. Sales happen, stock moves and small expenses add up. Without good records, it becomes hard to know what is working. Here are five simple habits to try.

1. Record every sale

Write down the date, item or service, quantity and price. A notebook can work at first, but a spreadsheet is easier to total and search later.

2. Keep stock separate from sales

A sale doesn't always tell you what is left on the shelf. Track what you bought, what you sold and what remains. Reconcile the numbers regularly.

3. Write down expenses too

Rent, deliveries, supplies, electricity and other costs matter. Revenue is not profit; subtract costs before deciding how much the business really earned.

4. Review the numbers weekly

Ask what sold most, what did not move, what customers asked for and whether stock or cash records need correcting. Look for patterns before making big decisions.

5. Use a simple dashboard when you're ready

A clear Excel dashboard can show sales totals, popular items, expenses and changes over time. The best dashboard is one you can keep updated without extra work.

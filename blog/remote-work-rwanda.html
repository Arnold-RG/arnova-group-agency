CAREERS · PRACTICAL GUIDE

Looking for remote work from Rwanda? Start here.

Remote doesn't always mean worldwide. A good application begins with checking the details.

Remote jobs can be a great opportunity, but many advertisements have country restrictions, specific working-hour requirements or rules about how you can be employed. Taking a few minutes to check those details can save you hours of applying to the wrong roles.

1. Read the location requirements first

Look for wording like “Remote — worldwide,” “Remote — EMEA,” or “must reside in” a named country. Even a job labelled remote may not accept applicants in Rwanda. If the job page is unclear, ask the employer before spending time on a long application.

2. Make your CV easy to understand

Put your actual experience, skills and relevant projects near the top. Keep job titles accurate and show what you did, rather than filling the page with buzzwords. Include a portfolio if it shows work you can explain in an interview.

3. Keep a simple application tracker

A spreadsheet with company, job title, link, date applied, status and follow-up date can stop you from applying twice or missing a response. You don't need expensive software to begin.

4. Watch for scams

  • Be careful if a supposed recruiter asks you to pay a fee to secure an interview.
  • Check that the email domain and job listing match the real company.
  • Don't share passwords or financial login details.
  • Be suspicious of guaranteed hiring or unusually high pay for little work.

5. Prepare for the interview, not just the application

Practice describing a real project or work situation. Employers want to understand how you solve problems, communicate and follow through.

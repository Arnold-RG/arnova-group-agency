# ArnovaGroup — Client Website Design Standard

## Principle: one business, one visual identity

ArnovaGroup's own brand palette is **not a default palette for client work**. Do not apply the same colors, same layout, or same website structure to every business found on Google Maps. Every site must feel like it belongs to its specific business.

This policy applies to website concepts, demo sites, client proposals, production builds, and future automation briefs for businesses in Rwanda and elsewhere.

## 1. Begin with the actual business, not a template

For each new lead, review the verified public business listing and relevant official sources to understand:
- Correct business name, category, location, opening hours (where verified), and provided contact methods.
- The business's official logo, brand colors, storefront signs, menu, uniforms, products, interior/exterior photographs, and official social profiles, where available.
- The customer's likely goal: book, call, WhatsApp, locate the business, view products/services, order, or request a quote.
- The audience and tone: local shop, restaurant, beauty salon, hotel, clinic, school, retail, professional service, etc.
- Actual content available, and what must be requested from the owner.

Do not assume a company has no website without verification. Clearly distinguish an **uncommissioned concept demo** from an official or authorized client site.

## 2. Derive the palette per client

Preferred order:
1. **Use approved brand assets from the business owner**, respecting the original logo and guidelines.
2. **If approved assets are unavailable**, use the verified business logo or publicly visible storefront/signage photos *as visual inspiration* for a proposed palette. Do not reproduce protected photos or artwork without permission.
3. **If there are no clear brand cues**, create a considered palette suited to the client's industry, audience, and character. Present it as a design suggestion, not a claim about the business's official identity.

For each client specify a primary, secondary, accent, background, text and CTA color with hex codes, plus light/dark usage and contrast tests. Choose the colors afresh; **no universal ArnovaGroup palette**.

Example directions only — never fixed templates:
- Café or bakery: warm espresso, oat, terracotta and restrained brass.
- Pharmacy or clinic: reassuring navy or teal with clean neutral backgrounds.
- Boutique or cosmetics: soft stone, aubergine, rose, olive, or a palette drawn from packaging.
- Hotel or travel: landscape-derived blues, warm sand and suitable premium accents.
- Automotive workshop: graphite, electric blue, amber or garage brand colors.
- Local grocery or supermarket: lively but legible produce-inspired colors, tied to signage.
These are prompts to explore, not mandatory category schemes.

## 3. Do not repeat the same page layout

Design around the business's *specific* user journey. Avoid repeating the same hero, grid, cards, color blocks, icon set, stock photo placement, typography, scroll effects and CTA arrangement across unrelated businesses.

Vary appropriately:
- Hero: full-bleed imagery, editorial split, centered search/menu, gallery, asymmetric typographic layout or product-led opening.
- Navigation and information architecture: products vs services vs rooms vs food menu vs bookings.
- Typography: choose distinct, readable pairs suited to the business's personality; preserve legibility in Kinyarwanda, English, French, or other requested languages.
- Sections: only include sections that make sense for that business. Don't invent testimonials, reviews, sales numbers, opening hours, offers, or contact information.
- Visual components: photography, cards, editorial text, product displays, map/contact views, galleries, room details, booking or menu links.
- Interaction: useful mobile actions (call, WhatsApp, map directions, menu, book), only if they actually work.

A small barbershop should not look like a travel agency or supermarket. Two restaurants should not become clones either.

## 4. Authentic imagery and facts

Google Maps listings and public websites are **research inputs, not an automatic license to copy content**. Prefer business-supplied or licensed images. Request permission to reuse third-party logos, listing images and customer photos in live projects. For initial concepts, use original illustrations, appropriately licensed visuals, or explicit placeholders. Attribute when required.

Never impersonate a business, claim official affiliation or publish a real business's site as its official website without authorization. Do not invent contact details, testimonials, ratings, awards, payment options, delivery availability, prices, opening hours or staff.

## 5. Client design brief (required for each website)

- Business: verified name, category, location and source link.
- Online presence: what was checked and what is missing or unknown.
- Distinctive cues: logo colors, signage, photography, products or atmosphere; mark observations vs assumptions.
- Concept: one sentence describing the unique creative direction.
- Palette: primary, secondary, accent, background, text, CTA (hex).
- Typography and composition: what makes this site distinguishable.
- First-screen message and primary action: tailored to this customer's likely visitor.
- Core sections (only needed sections), media permission status, mobile behavior and accessibility requirements.
- Review/approval status and implementation/deployment plan.

## 6. Quality gate before proposing or shipping

- [ ] Can this design be distinguished from other ArnovaGroup projects at first glance?
- [ ] Does the design visibly match the business's real identity, if one exists?
- [ ] Are layout, typography and section architecture business-specific?
- [ ] Are text and controls readable with accessible contrast on desktop and mobile?
- [ ] Does the primary CTA work, and are the links and details verified?
- [ ] Were photographs, logos and claims used only with appropriate permission?
- [ ] Is an unapproved concept clearly labeled as such?
- [ ] Is the client satisfied with the direction before production publishing?

**Bottom line:** ArnovaGroup is the studio behind the work, not the template that every customer must wear.
